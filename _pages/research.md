My current research is divided into two major arms. **1) What role does sensory processing play in mood disorders?** In this arm, I view sensory signals as anologous to the quantity and quality of information being input into neural systems. Neurocognitive aberrations are largely attributed to executive processes, but it remains unclear how much of a role early sensory processing systems play in disrupting these processes. In other terms, poor sensory signals representing one's active environment will likely translate to poor executive control. Work here is completed using behavioral tasks targeting sensation and neuroimaging datasets highlighting subcortical nuclei and somatosensory cortices. **2) How do top-down and bottom-up processes form stable states, and how is this process involved in mood disorders?** Within this arm, I am implemeting computational methodology to derive the latent structure of beliefs while participants perform tasks. The primary aim is identifying whether mood disorders are associated with altered dynamics between top-down states which possess predicted environmental states and bottom-up processing which communicates actualized environmental features, both extrinsic (e.g., stimulus qualia) and intrinsic (e.g., interoceptive visceral signals). In particular, I am interested in the robustness, or strength, of top-down and bottom-up signals in addition to other factors like volatility and stochasticity. In sum, the second arm of my research adopts a dynamical perspective for identifying neurobiological underpinnings of psychopathology. 

I have completed several projects during my graduate education, some published and many more relegated to the file drawer.
