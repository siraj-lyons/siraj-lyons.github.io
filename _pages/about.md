I am a 5th year Psychological Science PhD Candidate at the University of Louisville mentored by Dr. Brendan Depue. I study the neurobiological mechanisms of major depressive and bipolar disorder primarily using structural (i.e., T1w and DWI) and functional (i.e., functional connectivity/graph theory) MRI. Before enrolling at the University of Louisville, I conducted research using EEG/ERP methodology while earning a Master's of Science in Psychological Science at Northern Michigan University with Dr. Lin Fang. 

In my research, I conceptualize the brain as a closed-loop system emerging from complex interactions between bottom-up and top-down systems. Mood disorders are frequently investigated using motivational perspectives, but research often overlook sensorimotor components which allow for the detection of motivating cues and the execution of action to obtain rewards. Therefore, I am interested in the integration of reward and sensorimotor processing to construct "global" neurobiological models of major depressive and bipolar disorder. For example, my dissertation work has focused on midbrain and brainstem nuclei to explore the potential involvement of the ascending arousal network within mood disorders. Moreover, as cognition and mood are highly fluid and evolve over short timescales, I wish to extend my work in neuroscience by adopting continuous performance tasks, ecological momentary analysis, and computational modeling to enhance the detection of temporally dependent, bidirectional relationships between biology and behavior. The primary aim of this research is to identify the basic mechanisms of how the brain constructs predictive models of the environment, which are used to facilitate adaptive behavior, and isolate the potential casual mechanisms of mood disorders for clinical translation.  

At present, I am expecting to graduate in May 2027 and seeking for postdoctoral fellowship opportunities.
