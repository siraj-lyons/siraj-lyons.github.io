I am a 5th year Psychological Science PhD Candidate at the University of Louisville. My primary interests are in major depressive and bipolar disorders, with an emerging contingent concerned with psychosis spectrum disorders. I primarily focus on factors which contribute to aberrations of motivation found within mood disorders. While traversing through our lives, we use a combination of past experiences, whether positive or negative, alongside the existing sensory features of our surrounding environments to make decisions based upon our goals. Top-down and bottom-up processes are inextricably intertwined. What we've experienced biases our attention, which ultimately influences *what* and *how* we categorize the around us. Yet, the unexpected honking of a car or the display of a tasty treat demands our attention and encourages us to alter our behavior within a bottom-up fashion. Consequently, both systems are critical to explore when attempting to understand motivational aberrations which are typical among individuals experiencing an active mood episode (depression or hypo/mania). Research in this space typically investigates forebrain structures no more inferior than the thalamus. My work aims to broaden the scope by targeting midbrain and brainstem regions to explore their contributions to neurocognitive and motivational symptoms found in mood disorders. 

Adequate performance requires harmonization between extrinsic (e.g., environmental) and intrinsic (e.g., cognitive state) flexibility. Hyper-flexible cognitive states within stable environments over promotes learning and the detection of salient transitions, which leads to poor performance resembling stochastic responding. However, rigid states will miss salient cues within changing environments which should promote updating. It is in my view that investigating the accuracy to which individuals model the complexity of the environment while performing tasks is critical for understanding motivational and decision-making aberrations which are associated with mood disorders.
